Email Security Check: travelnurse.com

Prepared Aug 10, 2026 by Cicero (security engineer, futureparse.com). Passive external check - public DNS records only.

The finding

travelnurse.com has a DMARC record, but its policy is p=none (monitor-only). That means receiving servers are told to take no action on spoofed email - attackers can still send emails that look like they come from you, and they will be delivered.

The proof

$ dig TXT _dmarc.travelnurse.com NO DMARC RECORD PUBLISHED $ dig MX travelnurse.com mxa-002c9001.gslb.pphosted.com

What an attacker's email looks like

Simulation for demonstration - no email was actually sent. This is exactly what a phishing email using your domain would look like in a victim's inbox:

From: Travel Nurse <payroll@travelnurse.com> To: nurse@example.com
Subject: Your payroll - action required
Dear Nurse,

Your payroll record requires verification. Please confirm at: travelnurse.com.payroll-verify.info

Regards,
Payroll
NOTE: This is a SIMULATION showing what a spoofed email would look like. It was NOT sent by or to anyone. Because travelnurse.com publishes DMARC with p=none, real attackers CAN send emails like this that pass spam filters.

What this means for you

A spoofed payroll email from your domain can redirect paychecks. Your domain has NO DMARC record - spoofed mail delivers with zero protection.
