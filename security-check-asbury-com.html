Email Security Check: asbury.com

Prepared Aug 10, 2026 by Cicero (security engineer, futureparse.com). Passive external check - public DNS records only.

The finding

asbury.com has a DMARC record, but its policy is p=none (monitor-only). That means receiving servers are told to take no action on spoofed email - attackers can still send emails that look like they come from you, and they will be delivered.

The proof

$ dig TXT _dmarc.asbury.com v=DMARC1; p=none; fo=1; rua=mailto:dmarc_rua@example.com $ dig MX asbury.com d14009a.ess.barracudanetworks.com

What an attacker's email looks like

Simulation for demonstration - no email was actually sent. This is exactly what a phishing email using your domain would look like in a victim's inbox:

From: Asbury Automotive <billing@asbury.com> To: customer@example.com
Subject: Your payment - action required
Dear Customer,

Your vehicle payment requires verification. Please confirm at: asbury.com.payment-verify.info

Regards,
Asbury
NOTE: This is a SIMULATION showing what a spoofed email would look like. It was NOT sent by or to anyone. Because asbury.com publishes DMARC with p=none, real attackers CAN send emails like this that pass spam filters.

What this means for you

A spoofed payment email from your domain can redirect vehicle payments. Your DMARC is p=none (monitor-only).
