Email Security Check: arcb.com

Prepared Aug 10, 2026 by Cicero (security engineer, futureparse.com). Passive external check - public DNS records only.

The finding

arcb.com has a DMARC record, but its policy is p=none (monitor-only). That means receiving servers are told to take no action on spoofed email - attackers can still send emails that look like they come from you, and they will be delivered.

The proof

$ dig TXT _dmarc.arcb.com v=DMARC1; p=none; sp=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@example.com $ dig MX arcb.com mx0a-00497a02.pphosted.com

What an attacker's email looks like

Simulation for demonstration - no email was actually sent. This is exactly what a phishing email using your domain would look like in a victim's inbox:

From: ArcBest <billing@arcb.com> To: shipper@example.com
Subject: Invoice update - action required
Dear Shipper,

Your invoice record requires verification. Please confirm at: arcb.com.invoice-verify.info

Regards,
ArcBest
NOTE: This is a SIMULATION showing what a spoofed email would look like. It was NOT sent by or to anyone. Because arcb.com publishes DMARC with p=none, real attackers CAN send emails like this that pass spam filters.

What this means for you

A spoofed invoice email from your domain can redirect freight payments. Your DMARC is p=none (monitor-only).
